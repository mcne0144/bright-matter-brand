# The Heist — 60-second spot

A woman walks into a marketing agency and takes back what was hers the whole
time. Tongue in cheek, fearless, no apologies. Eight scenes, 9:16 vertical,
built with AI generation and a dubbed voiceover.

- `script.html` — the production page. Same content as below, with copy buttons on every prompt. Open it in a browser while you work.
- `prompts.md` — just the prompts, nothing else, for fast copy-paste.
- This file — the full script, readable on GitHub.

## Why it works

**Every joke is a value prop.** She never steals anything random. Each item is
something agencies actually withhold — the dashboard, the accounts, the customer
list. The fridge gag is the one pure joke, and it earns its place by being the
only one.

**The pain is real.** Agencies holding client data hostage is a documented,
widely-searched frustration. Satire lands hardest when the audience has lived it.

**Raw beats polished.** Founder-fronted short-form currently outperforms
high-budget production. A dubbed voice over AI footage is the format, not a
compromise.

## How to build it

Two steps per scene:

1. **Start frame.** Run the image prompt with the founder photo attached as
   character reference (Soul 2.0 or Nano Banana Pro). Every prompt opens with
   the same character description — keep it identical so she never drifts
   between clips.
2. **Animate.** Feed the approved frame to image-to-video (Kling 3.0) with the
   motion prompt. 5–8 seconds, 9:16.

**No spoken dialogue on camera.** The voiceover is dubbed, so every mouth stays
closed. The one "spoken" line in scene 5 plays as a dead stare into the lens.
Funnier that way, and it sidesteps lip-sync entirely.

**Consistency kit:** same wardrobe in every prompt; cool grey agency interiors
so her warm cream suit reads as the invader; the same 35mm lens language and
film grain throughout.

## Scene 1 — The doors · 6s

Cold open. She pushes through the glass doors mid-stride, slow motion, sunlight
flaring behind her. The receptionist's smile dies.

**Image prompt**
> Editorial cinematic photo, 9:16. A confident woman with long wavy auburn red hair and freckles, tailored cream suit, gold necklace, pushing open tall glass doors into a sleek pretentious marketing agency lobby with cool grey walls, neon sign reading SYNERGY, a startled receptionist behind a white desk. She is mid-stride, sunglasses on, lit by warm sunlight flaring behind her, agency interior in cold blue-grey tones. 35mm, shallow depth, film grain.

**Motion prompt**
> Slow motion confident walk toward camera as the glass doors swing shut behind her, her hair and blazer moving with the stride, receptionist turning to watch in alarm, subtle camera dolly backward, cinematic, stable face.

**VO** — *cool, matter-of-fact*
> This is a marketing agency. Somewhere in this building is everything they know about my customers. I'm here to take it back.

**Overlay:** none yet. Let the walk breathe.

## Scene 2 — The dashboard · 7s

She stops at a huge wall monitor labeled CLIENT DASHBOARD, admires it for half a
beat, then yanks the cable. Screen dies. An account manager's jaw drops.

**Image prompt**
> Editorial cinematic photo, 9:16. A confident woman with long wavy auburn red hair and freckles, tailored cream suit, gold necklace, standing in a modern agency office beside a huge wall-mounted monitor showing colorful marketing analytics charts labeled CLIENT DASHBOARD. She grips the monitor's power cable, about to pull it. Behind her a panicked young account manager in a black turtleneck holds an iced coffee. Cold grey office, warm light on her, 35mm, film grain.

**Motion prompt**
> She yanks the cable out with one clean pull, the screen goes black, she tucks the coiled cable under her arm like a handbag and walks off. The account manager flinches and spills a little coffee. Camera static, deadpan comedy timing, stable face.

**VO** — *dry*
> That's my dashboard. I've paid for it monthly for three years. I've seen it twice.

**Overlay:** the checklist begins, top-left — "your data ✓".

**Watch for:** the monitor text must read CLIENT DASHBOARD legibly, and she must
be holding the cable in the still — the animation needs that grip to pull from.

## Scene 3 — The pyramid · 7s

Glass conference room. A strategist is presenting a triangle diagram. She walks
in without knocking, takes the marker from his hand mid-gesture, and leaves.

**Image prompt**
> Editorial cinematic photo, 9:16. Inside a glass-walled agency conference room, a smug strategist in designer glasses presents a whiteboard with a large triangle diagram labeled BRAND PYRAMID to bored executives. A confident woman with long wavy auburn red hair and freckles, tailored cream suit, gold necklace, reaches in from the doorway taking the marker out of his raised hand. Cold office tones, warm light on her, 35mm, film grain.

**Motion prompt**
> She plucks the marker from his hand without breaking stride and exits frame, he freezes mid-sentence with his hand still raised, the executives look at each other, one slow blink. Camera static, dry comedy, stable faces.

**VO** — *drier*
> They're presenting my brand pyramid. I never asked for a pyramid.

**Alt VO, harder jab:** "Four people billed forty hours for this triangle."

**Watch for:** pick the frame where their two hands connect on the marker — the
frozen handoff is the gag. The comedy is his stillness, not her speed; if the
animation over-reacts, add "he does not move, only his eyes follow her."

## Scene 4 — The vault · 8s

The heist beat. Server room in blue light. She unplugs a cable tagged YOUR DATA,
coils it over her shoulder like climbing rope, and lifts a box holding a hard
drive labeled CUSTOMER LIST.

**Image prompt**
> Editorial cinematic photo, 9:16. A confident woman with long wavy auburn red hair and freckles, tailored cream suit, gold necklace, standing in a dark server room lit blue, pulling a glowing ethernet cable with a paper tag reading YOUR DATA from a rack, a cardboard box at her feet holding a hard drive labeled CUSTOMER LIST and a trophy. Dramatic heist lighting, warm key light on her face, 35mm, film grain.

**Motion prompt**
> She unplugs the cable in slow motion, coils it over her shoulder like a rope, picks up the cardboard box, and strides toward camera while red warning lights start blinking on the racks behind her. Confident, unhurried, cinematic heist energy, stable face.

**VO** — *picking up pace*
> My data. My ad accounts. My customer list. Funny how "proprietary" always means mine, but they keep it.

**Overlay:** checklist grows — "your data ✓ / your ad accounts ✓ / your customer list ✓".

**Watch for:** the YOUR DATA tag is the money shot — regenerate until it's
legible. Keep the warm key light on her face against the blue room; if she comes
out as blue as the servers, rerun.

## Scene 5 — The fridge · 5s

**The** joke. Break room. She takes a labeled lunch container and the oat milk,
then looks dead into the camera. Hold. Hold. Walk.

**Image prompt**
> Editorial cinematic photo, 9:16. A confident woman with long wavy auburn red hair and freckles, tailored cream suit, gold necklace, standing at an open office fridge in a trendy agency break room, holding a glass meal-prep container with a sticky note reading GREG and a carton of oat milk, looking directly into the camera with a completely deadpan expression. Kombucha bottles on the shelf, cold office light, warm light on her face, 35mm, film grain.

**Motion prompt**
> She closes the fridge door with her hip, holds a dead-serious stare into the lens for two full seconds without blinking, then walks out of frame with the lunch container and oat milk. Deadpan, no smile until the very last frame where one corner of her mouth twitches. Stable face.

**VO** — *flat. do not smile.*
> I'm taking this too.

**Overlay:** "greg's lunch ✓" joins the checklist. Put the beat drop here.

**Watch for:** the stare is everything. Her eyes must be locked on the lens, zero
expression. Any hint of a smile in the start frame — regenerate. The mouth-corner
twitch belongs only in the video's final frames. The VO is 1.8s in a 5s clip:
place it on the look, then let three seconds play in silence. The silence is the joke.

## Scene 6 — The panic · 6s

Wide shot. The open office descends into chaos behind her calm exit walk.

**Image prompt**
> Editorial cinematic photo, 9:16. A confident woman with long wavy auburn red hair and freckles, tailored cream suit, gold necklace, carrying a cardboard box of office items including a coiled cable, a framed motivational poster and a small plant, walking calmly through a chaotic open-plan agency office. Behind her: a man in a beanie on the phone gesturing wildly, a woman pointing at a dead wall monitor, sticky notes falling off a glass wall. She is serene, they are not. 35mm, film grain.

**Motion prompt**
> She walks screen-left to right calm and unbothered while the office panics behind her in layered background action, papers drifting, phone gesturing, one person slowly sinking into a beanbag in despair. Camera tracks her at walking pace, comedy in the background, stable face.

**VO** — *the turn, conspiratorial*
> Here's what they don't want you to know. AI can now run most of what they charge six figures a year to babysit.

**Alt VO, meaner:** "Nobody in this office knows your customers. They know your budget."

**Overlay:** "$200,000/yr retainer" with a strikethrough animating across it.

**Watch for:** the gag lives in the background — at least two panicking people
must be clearly visible, or rerun.

## Scene 7 — The handoff · 7s

Outside. Golden hour. She drops the box in the trunk, closes it, leans back.
The sincere beat: the offer.

**Image prompt**
> Editorial cinematic photo, 9:16. A confident woman with long wavy auburn red hair and freckles, tailored cream suit, gold necklace, leaning against the closed trunk of a vintage car parked outside a glass office building at golden hour, arms crossed, warm sunset light, the cold agency building looming behind her. Relaxed, victorious, slight knowing smile. 35mm, film grain.

**Motion prompt**
> Gentle push-in toward her as she leans on the trunk, wind moves her hair, she gives a small confident nod to camera, golden hour flare drifting across the frame. Warm, calm, sincere. Stable face.

**VO** — *warm, sincere, slower*
> You know your business better than any agency ever will. I build the automated systems: content, reporting, follow-up. Your team runs them. Your data stays yours.

**Overlay:** none. Sincerity plays clean.

**Watch for:** the only scene where she's allowed to look warm. Don't accept a
deadpan frame here. The VO runs 12.4s against a 7s clip — let it carry into the
end card. The offer landing over the brand mark is the intended close.

## Scene 8 — The card · 4s

**Don't generate this one.** Build it in the editor: `brand/orbit-mark.png` on

"Plan my transition."

**VO** — *the button*

## The voiceover

About 105 words total. Read dry and unhurried, roughly 140 words per minute, and
let the pauses do the acting. The only line with any warmth is scene 7.

Generated VO (one file per scene) runs against clip lengths like this:

| Scene | Clip | VO |
|---|---|---|
| 1 doors | 6s | 8.4s |
| 2 dashboard | 7s | 6.9s |
| 3 pyramid | 7s | 8.2s |
| 4 vault | 8s | 9.8s |
| 5 fridge | 5s | 1.8s |
| 6 panic | 6s | 7.2s |
| 7 handoff | 7s | 12.4s |
| 8 card | 4s | 6.9s |

Lines running past their clip is normal — let the voice spill across the cut.
That overlap (a J-cut) makes the edit feel more professional, not less.

## The 15-second teaser

No new generation. Re-edit four existing clips:

1. **0:00–0:03** — scene 5, the fridge. Open cold on the stare, zero context.
2. **0:03–0:06** — scene 2, the cable yank.
3. **0:06–0:11** — scene 4, the vault, slow motion.
4. **0:11–0:15** — scene 8, the logo card.

**Teaser VO (32 words):** "I'm taking this too. They held my data hostage for
three years. So I took it back. And AI runs it for a fraction of the retainer.
Bright Matter. Take your marketing in-house."

## Spare gags

- **The vinyl wall.** She peels the giant DISRUPT lettering off one letter at a time and keeps only the D. *"I'll leave them the rest."*
- **The ping-pong table.** She takes one paddle mid-rally and keeps walking. The other player holds their serve forever.
- **The invoice.** She turns a framed RETAINER invoice face-down at reception, pats it twice like a casket.
- **The plant.** *"The ficus was billed as a creative expense. The ficus comes with me."*
- **Cold-open variant.** Start on the fridge stare, then cut to the doors. The confusion resolves at the title card.
