BRIGHT MATTER

The Heist.

A 60-second spot. One woman walks into a marketing agency and takes back what was hers the whole time. Tongue in cheek, fearless, zero apologies. You dub the voiceover; the video carries the bit.

Format9:16 vertical, Reels / TikTok / Shorts
Runtime~60s full cut, 30s cut marked below
Build8 clips, ~5 to 8s each, in Higgsfield
AudioYour dubbed VO over a sly heist-jazz track

Why this works

Three rules the research handed us

Every joke is a value prop

The Dollar Shave Club rule. She never steals anything random: each item she takes is a thing agencies actually withhold. The fridge gag is the one pure joke, and it earns it.

The pain is real

"Agency holds your data hostage" is a documented, searched-for frustration: locked ad accounts, dashboards you pay for but never see, "proprietary" everything. Satire lands hardest when the audience has lived it.

Raw beats polished

Founder-fronted, slightly imperfect short-form is outperforming big-budget gloss right now. Your own dubbed voice over AI-shot footage is the format, not a compromise.

How to build it in Higgsfield

The two-step per scene

  • Step 1: generate the start frame. Use your reference photo (or your trained Soul) with Soul 2.0 or Nano Banana Pro so the heroine is you in every scene. Every image prompt below opens with the same character token; keep it identical so the woman never drifts between clips.
  • Step 2: animate it. Feed each start frame to your preferred video model as image-to-video with the motion prompt. Ask for 5 to 8 seconds, 9:16.
  • No spoken dialogue on camera. You are dubbing, so keep every mouth closed. The one "spoken" line in scene 5 plays as a dead stare into the lens while your VO says it. Funnier that way anyway.
  • Consistency kit: same wardrobe token in every prompt, same office palette (warm wood, glass, cool grey agency-chic so YOUR warm cream reads as the invader), same lens language.

Spare parts

Alternate gags and hooks

  • Cold-open variant for the 30s cut: start ON the fridge stare with "I'm taking this too," THEN cut to the doors. Scroll-stopping in the first second, and the confusion resolves at the title card.
  • The vinyl wall: she peels the giant DISRUPT vinyl lettering off the wall one letter at a time and keeps only the D. VO: "I'll leave them the rest."
  • The ping-pong table: she takes one paddle mid-rally and keeps walking. The other player just holds their serve forever.
  • The invoice: she picks up a framed invoice from reception labeled RETAINER, turns it face-down gently, pats it twice like a casket.
  • The plant: "The ficus was billed as a creative expense. The ficus comes with me."
