Why this, why now

Fifty-plus stores are posting. We expect to find no shared standard.

You already have what most dealer groups lack. Luther has been family owned since 1952, carries 27 brands, and runs its own marketing center. The people, the stories and the production talent are already in the building.

The gap we expect to find is the system around them. When each store decides alone what to post, how often, and what counts as on brand, quality depends on who happens to hold the login that month. Risk does too: a payment or lease offer a store posts is still an advertisement, and the disclosure rules apply to it.

So the scope is deliberately narrow. Organic social only, and an audit of your group pages plus a sample of 8 to 10 stores chosen across brands, markets and store sizes. A sample shows the patterns in weeks. Auditing every rooftop would take months and tell us the same thing.

From there, one written standard goes to every store through your own team.

This is our read from the outside and it is a hypothesis. The first job of the sprint is to test it against your pages and your people, and to say so plainly if it is wrong.

What we're actually doing

In plain terms, no jargon.

Weeks 1 to 3

We look

We read your group pages and a sample of 8 to 10 store pages, post by post. We talk to the people who run them and to a few general managers. We write down what is working, what is missing, and where stores contradict each other.

Weeks 4 to 6

We decide and build

We write the strategy, the governance playbook and the content pillars. We draft the KPI framework with your leadership, using the numbers they already care about.

Weeks 7 to 8

We present it

We walk your leadership through all of it in one readout. You leave with the documents, a 12-month roadmap, and a clear choice about Phase 2.

Month 3 onward

We run it with your people

We take the standard to the stores through your marketing center. We train the people who post, review what goes out, and report to leadership every month. How much of this we carry depends on the tier you choose.

Measuring success

Your leadership sets the scoreboard before rollout starts.

We do not arrive with a list of KPIs. During the sprint we ask your marketing leadership and a sample of general managers what organic social should do for a Luther store, in their words.

Those answers become the KPI framework. It is signed off at the gate, before any Phase 2 fee is paid, and it is the only scoreboard the monthly briefing reports against.

If a number cannot be measured from data you own, it does not go on the scoreboard.

How the scoreboard gets built

  • Interviews with marketing leadership and sample store GMs
  • A short list of KPIs, each tied to a data source you control
  • A baseline taken from the audit
  • Sign-off at the Phase 1 gate
  • Review against the baseline at month 6
