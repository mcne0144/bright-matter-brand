The vocabulary

How to ask for everything
you just scrolled past.

Each card names a technique used on this page and gives you the exact phrase to drop into a prompt. Toggle the orange annotations off (bottom-right) to see the page the way a visitor would.

Scroll-reveal fade-up

Content fades in and rises a short distance as it enters the viewport. The default "alive" feeling; almost every modern site uses it.

"Fade sections up ~20px as they enter the viewport, 600ms, ease-out, triggered once."

Stagger sequencing

Sibling elements (cards, list items) animate one after another instead of all at once. Small delays read as choreography.

"Stagger the cards 80–100ms apart so they cascade in left to right."

Parallax depth

Background layers scroll slower than the foreground, creating depth. Keep the ratio subtle — 0.2x to 0.5x — or it feels like 2014.

"Give the background a subtle parallax — it should move at about 0.3x scroll speed behind the content."

Scroll-scrubbing scroll-linked

An animation's progress is tied directly to scroll position — scroll down, it advances; scroll up, it reverses. Distinct from time-based animation.

"Tie the image's scale to scroll position: it settles from 1.08 to 1.0 as it crosses the viewport."

Pinned / sticky section position: sticky

One element holds still while content scrolls past it — a headline that anchors a chapter, or a panel that swaps content as you scroll.

"Pin the section headline on the left while the steps scroll past on the right."

Micro-interaction hover / press

Tiny responses to input: a card lifting on hover, a link drawing its underline, a button dimpling on press. This is where "premium" lives.

"On hover, lift cards 4px with a softer, larger shadow — 250ms ease-out. Links animate their underline left to right."

Page-load sequence entrance

The hero builds itself on arrival — headline, then copy, then buttons. One orchestrated moment beats effects scattered everywhere.

"On load, sequence the hero in: eyebrow, headline, copy, CTAs — each 100ms apart, rising 24px."

Count-up number ticker

Stats animate from zero to their value when they first become visible. Draws the eye to proof points exactly once.

"Count the stats up from 0 over ~1.2s the first time they scroll into view."

Condensing nav scroll state

The navigation starts tall and transparent, then shrinks and gains a blurred, translucent background once you scroll.

"Make the nav sticky: transparent at top, then condensed with a backdrop blur and hairline shadow after ~40px of scroll."

Marquee auto-scroll

A strip of logos or names that scrolls continuously. Loops seamlessly, pauses on hover. Good for partners, locations, press.

"Add an infinite marquee of the store names between sections — slow, seamless loop, pause on hover."

Easing & duration the feel

The difference between elegant and janky. Ease-out for entrances, 200–400ms for micro-interactions, 500–700ms for reveals. Nothing bounces unless the brand is playful.

"All motion ease-out, micro-interactions 200–300ms, reveals ~600ms. Subtle — nothing bouncy or springy."

Reduced motion accessibility

Some visitors get motion sick. A well-built site turns its animation off for them automatically — and asking for it signals you know what you're doing.

"Respect prefers-reduced-motion: show everything static, no parallax or reveals, for users who've turned animation off."
